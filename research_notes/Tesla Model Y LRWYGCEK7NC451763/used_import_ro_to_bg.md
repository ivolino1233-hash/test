# Buying this used car in Romania and registering it in Bulgaria

Method note: researched 2026-10-12. WebFetch and curl could not reach any site from this environment (DNS failure on every host, including tesla.com, NHTSA, autoDNA and carVertical), so **every fact below comes from search-engine results and snippets, not from pages I opened**. Second research pass; the first pass ran out of shared search budget, so this pass re-checked or replaced its claims. Searches used: 25.

## Takeaway

I used all 25 of my searches. Each finding below says whether this run's web sources support it. On VAT, the official EU sources (Your Europe and DG TAXUD) agree that a car is still 'new' if it is no more than 6 months old OR has done no more than 6,000 km. Only a car past both limits is 'used'. Your Europe states plainly that a used car bought from a private person carries no VAT in either country. From a dealer, VAT may or may not appear on the invoice, depending on whether the dealer uses the margin scheme. Romania's standard VAT rate is 21% from 1 Aug 2025 (Law 141/2025), and 2026 guides still give 21%; a rise to 23% in 2026 was denied by the finance minister. Romanian provisional (red) plates are a dead end whoever applies for them: the Romanian Border Police say they are valid only on Romanian territory. That leaves Bulgarian 'T' transit plates (€40.90 for one month, issued only by KAT, MTPL required; it is still unconfirmed whether the car must be shown in Bulgaria first) or a carrier. Romanian deregistration for export is requested by the registered owner. The file holds the local-tax-office fiscal certificate, the registration certificate, plates, the CIV and proof of export: the foreign sale contract (with a certified translation if it is in a foreign language) or proof of registration abroad. It can go ahead without the CIV and plates if the owner proves a foreign authority kept them, which supports 'register in Bulgaria first, the seller deregisters afterwards'. MVR's official first-registration page corrects round 1 on inspection. The car must be shown in person; KAT takes the foreign valid inspection document, and if there is none or it has expired, the technical check is done at the 'Пътна полиция' point. A separate ГТП is therefore not clearly mandatory (one secondary site says it is for cars over 3 years old). MVR also requires: the foreign registration certificate (parts I and II if issued), proof of ownership, a declaration of acquisition and proof the product fee was paid for M1 cars. Its documents mention a legalized Bulgarian translation for foreign documents and the CoC. The law (ZDvP art. 140) requires registration within 1 month of acquisition. Round 1 was wrong that EVs pay no eco/product fee. Since 1 Jul 2025 EVs pay it: 102 BGN for a new EV, and about 180–188 BGN (~€92–96) reported for a used EV up to 5 years old. A bill to abolish the fee at KAT registration passed first reading in Sep 2026 but, per Capital (30 Sep 2026), still awaited second reading. Annual vehicle tax for a BEV is €0 in practically all municipalities, but sources disagree whether that comes from the law or from municipal ordinances. Round 1 missed a possibly significant cost: the municipal acquisition tax (данък при придобиване), 0.1–3% of the insurance value or price as each municipality sets it. Its 2-month declaration period starts from first Bulgarian registration for a car not previously registered in Bulgaria. Whether it applies to an EU import was not confirmed. Not found this run: the MVR plate and certificate fee in EUR (round 1's €31.42 is unverified), a Model Y MTPL premium, Bucharest–Sofia carrier prices, and any Bulgarian green EV plate scheme (none found). My estimate is roughly €200–700 in fixed fees and logistics, plus MTPL and a possible acquisition tax of 0.1–3% of value, and about 1–3 weeks.

## Cited findings

### 1. VAT – new vs used test (high confidence, sourced in this pass)

For VAT a car counts as a 'new means of transport' if it has done no more than 6,000 km OR is no more than 6 months past first entry into service. Your Europe defines a used car as one that has done more than 6,000 km AND was supplied more than 6 months after first registration, so both limits must be passed. Only new vehicles trigger VAT in the destination country (in Bulgaria, 20% declared to NAP).

- **This car:** Built about Aug 2022 and first registered about Sep 2022, so it is about 4 years past first entry into service. It is 'used' for VAT unless the odometer shows 6,000 km or less, which is very unlikely; check the reading. No Bulgarian VAT and no NAP declaration.
- **Sources:** [europa.eu](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/vehicles/cars/vat-on-cars-bought-abroad/index_en.htm), [taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu](https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/buying-and-selling-cars_en), [europa.eu](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/vehicles/cars/vat-buying-selling-cars/faq/index_en.htm)

### 2. VAT – private seller (high confidence, sourced in this pass)

Your Europe: a used car bought from a private person carries no VAT, either in the country of purchase or in the country of registration. Registration fees still apply in the country of residence.

- **This car:** If a private Romanian owner sells, the agreed price is the whole tax cost: no VAT in Romania or Bulgaria.
- **Sources:** [europa.eu](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/vehicles/cars/vat-on-cars-bought-abroad/index_en.htm), [evropskyspotrebitel.gov.cz](https://evropskyspotrebitel.gov.cz/faq-car-purchases-in-other-eu-countries)

### 3. VAT – dealer sale (margin scheme vs normal VAT) (medium confidence, sourced in this pass)

Your Europe FAQ: on a used car from a dealer, VAT 'may or may not be separately mentioned on the invoice', depending on how the dealer calculates VAT (margin scheme vs normal regime). Trade sources describe the margin scheme as having VAT included in the price, not shown separately. Round 1's detail on the Romanian Fiscal Code art. 312 wording ('regim special – bunuri second-hand') was not verified this run.

- **This car:** If a Romanian dealer sells, ask before paying whether the invoice is margin scheme (no VAT shown, final price) or normal regime (21% Romanian VAT on top).
- **Sources:** [europa.eu](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/vehicles/cars/vat-buying-selling-cars/faq/index_en.htm), [ecarstrade.com](https://ecarstrade.com/blog/how-vat-works-when-buying-used-cars)

### 4. Romanian VAT rate 2026 (high confidence, sourced in this pass)

Law 141/2025 (published 25 Jul 2025) raised Romania's standard VAT from 19% to 21% from 1 Aug 2025 and created a single reduced rate of 11%. 2026 tax guides still give 21%. The finance minister denied a rise to 23% in 2026. No ANAF notice dated Oct 2026 was found.

- **This car:** An ex-company or ex-lease car invoiced under the normal regime costs the net price +21% to a private Bulgarian buyer. For a used car this VAT is a final cost; the new-car refund route does not apply (background reasoning, not re-verified).
- **Sources:** [ey.com](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/romanian-tax-changes-introduced-by-new-fiscal-and-budgetary-measures), [forvismazars.com](https://www.forvismazars.com/ro/en/insights/publications/economic-publications/forvis-mazars-cee-tax-guide-2026), [trykintsugi.com](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/europe/romania), [vatcalc.com](https://www.vatcalc.com/romania/romania-mulls-further-2-vat-increase-in-2026/)

### 5. Romanian provisional (red) plates (high confidence, sourced in this pass)

The Romanian Border Police state that the provisional authorisation (red plates) allows driving only on Romanian territory. Guides give validity as 30 days (+60-day extension), or 30/60/90 days if covered by RCA, and the fee as about 53 RON. The file is the application, an RCA policy in the owner's name, proof of ownership and ID. Whether a non-resident Bulgarian can get them was not confirmed.

- **This car:** Do not plan to drive the car into Bulgaria on Romanian red plates: they are not valid past the border whoever obtains them. Use a carrier or Bulgarian transit plates.
- **Sources:** [politiadefrontiera.ro](https://www.politiadefrontiera.ro/ro/main/i-este-posibila-trecerea-frontierei-cu-un-vehicul-care-circula-cu-numere-de-inmatriculare-provizorii-7527.html), [cumobtin.ro](https://cumobtin.ro/numere-proviorii/), [asigurari.ro](https://www.asigurari.ro/asigurare/rca/numere-rosii), [avocatnet.ro](https://www.avocatnet.ro/forum/discutie_251314/Circulatia-in-UE-si-in-afara-UE-cu-numere-de-inmatriculare-provizorii.html)

### 6. Romanian deregistration (radiere) for export (medium confidence, sourced in this pass)

Export deregistration applies when the car is sold to a buyer in another country. It is two steps. (1) The fiscal certificate (certificat de atestare fiscală) from the local tax office, with all taxes and fines paid and the car removed from the tax record. (2) An application to the registration service (DRPCIV, renamed DGPCI per one guide) with the application form, the original registration certificate, plates, ID, the original CIV plus a copy, the fiscal certificate, and proof of export. Proof of export is the foreign sale contract (with a certified translation if it is in a foreign language) or the document of registration in the destination state. One guide says deregistration can go ahead without the CIV and plates if the owner proves a foreign authority kept them. The registered owner (the seller) files it; one guide says the buyer 'usually' takes care of registration in their own country.

- **This car:** Two sequences look possible. (A) The seller deregisters before handover using the bilingual contract and gives you the CIV; the car then moves by truck or on Bulgarian 'T' plates. (B) You register in Bulgaria first with the original RO certificate and CIV, KAT keeps them, and the seller deregisters with a copy of your Bulgarian certificate. Write the sequence and who does what into the sale contract. All sources are commercial guides, not official DGPCI text.
- **Sources:** [carmatik.ro](https://www.carmatik.ro/ghid-auto-carmatik/radiere-auto-pentru-export/), [wall-street.ro](https://www.wall-street.ro/articol/Auto/cum-radiezi-o-masina.html), [dosar-auto.ro](https://www.dosar-auto.ro/ro/radierea-autoturismului/), [edy-automobile.ro](https://edy-automobile.ro/radiere-in-cazul-exportului), [inmatriculariauto.eu](https://www.inmatriculariauto.eu/servicii/radiere-autovehicul/), [carmatik.ro](https://www.carmatik.ro/ghid-auto-carmatik/scoatere-fiscal-masina-auto/)

### 7. Bulgarian transit ('T') plates (medium confidence, sourced in this pass)

Bulgarian transit plates are issued only by KAT for moving a vehicle between countries on import or export. One provider's price list gives €40.90 for one month. Validity is about 1 month, extendable (up to 2 times, after showing the car at KAT, per one provider). They need valid MTPL ('Гражданска отговорност'), a technically sound car and, for an import, the ownership documents from the country of origin. No source confirmed whether KAT will issue them for a car that is still physically in Romania.

- **This car:** Possible only if KAT confirms in advance that it will issue T plates for a car in Romania. Otherwise, or if KAT refuses, a carrier is the reliable route. Snippet-level sources only.
- **Sources:** [avtoekspert.com](https://avtoekspert.com/2026/06/30/tranzitni-nomera/), [bulcars.bg](https://www.bulcars.bg/%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B7%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%BD%D0%B8-%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B0-%D1%86%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%B8-2025/), [bmwpower-bg.net](https://bmwpower-bg.net/forums/viewtopic.php?p=4307189&sid=212782fa27d0e7705dcee22a4a09ec8e), [advice.bg](https://advice.bg/n-140944-%D0%9A%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B2%D0%B0_%D0%B5_%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%BE%D1%86%D0%B5%D0%B4%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0_%D0%B7%D0%B0_%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B7%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%BD%D0%B8_%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B0)

### 8. Bulgarian first registration – documents and inspection (MVR) (medium confidence, sourced in this pass)

Per the official MVR first-registration service page and the MVR document list under Ordinance I-45, the owner presents the vehicle in person. The file is: a written application; the registration certificate from the other member state (parts I and II if issued); the document of acquisition (contract or invoice); a declaration of acquisition; and, for M1/N1 and some L categories, proof the product fee was paid. If the vehicle has a valid roadworthiness document from an EU/EEA/Swiss state, it is presented. If there is none or it has expired, the technical-condition check is done at the 'Пътна полиция' registration point. A secondary 2026 site says a valid ГТП certificate is mandatory for used imports over 3 years old, and another says no separate ГТП is needed because the KAT check gives a 1-year sticker. ZDvP art. 140 requires vehicles to be presented for registration within 1 month of acquisition.

- **This car:** Bring the car itself, the original RO registration certificate and CIV, the contract/invoice, your ID, product-fee proof and MTPL. Also bring the latest Romanian ITP certificate: a Sep-2022 car's first Romanian ITP would have fallen around 2025 (background, not verified). If it is valid, it may replace or shorten the KAT check. Ask the local KAT whether it wants a separate ГТП. Register within 1 month of purchase.
- **Sources:** [mvr.bg](https://mvr.bg/opp/%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%BC%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B2%D0%BD%D0%B8-%D1%83%D1%81%D0%BB%D1%83%D0%B3%D0%B8/%D1%83%D1%81%D0%BB%D1%83%D0%B3%D0%B8-%D0%BF%D0%BE-%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B3%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B0%D1%86%D0%B8%D1%8F-%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BF%D1%81/%D0%B8%D0%B7%D0%B2%D1%8A%D1%80%D1%88%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B5-%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BF%D1%8A%D1%80%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B3%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%86%D0%B8%D1%8F-%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%B7%D0%BD%D0%BE-%D1%81%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B4%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%BE), [mvr.bg](https://mvr.bg/upload/21666/62a323c3-dokumenti_za_reg_mps2pdf.pdf), [mvr.bg](https://mvr.bg/upload/19948/naredba-i45-ot-24-mart-2000-g-za-registrirane-otc_22.pdf), [mvr.bg](https://mvr.bg/upload/15048/1239-%D0%B8%D0%B7%D0%B2%D1%8A%D1%80%D1%88%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B5-%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BF%D1%8A%D1%80%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%87%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B3%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%86%D0%B8%D1%8F-%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%BE%D0%B7%D0%BD%D0%BE-%D1%81%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B4%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%BE.docx), [selectauto.bg](https://www.selectauto.bg/blog/registratsiya-na-vnesena-kola-stapka-po-stapka), [novinata.bg](https://novinata.bg/bulgaria/registraciq-na-avtomobil-ot-chujbina-v-balgariq-dokumenti-stapki-i-vajni-podrobnosti/)

### 9. Translation of Romanian documents (medium confidence, sourced in this pass)

MVR's registration document list says foreign documents are presented with a legalized translation into Bulgarian ('легализиран превод'). The manufacturer's certificate of conformity (CoC) must be in Bulgarian or come with a legalized translation. The sources do not say whether this applies to EU-harmonised registration certificates. No source mentions an apostille for EU documents.

- **This car:** Budget a legalized translation (a translation agency licensed by the Bulgarian MFA) of the Romanian sale contract/invoice, and of the CoC if one is presented. A bilingual RO/BG contract may reduce this. Confirm with the KAT office.
- **Sources:** [mvr.bg](https://mvr.bg/upload/21666/62a323c3-dokumenti_za_reg_mps2pdf.pdf), [mvr.bg](https://mvr.bg/upload/19948/naredba-i45-ot-24-mart-2000-g-za-registrirane-otc_22.pdf)

### 10. Bulgarian product fee ('екотакса') – applies to EVs since 1 Jul 2025 (medium confidence, sourced in this pass)

Round 1 said EVs pay no eco/product fee; that is outdated. From 1 Jul 2025 the product-fee tariff was raised: about 150 BGN for a new petrol/diesel M1, 105 BGN for a hybrid and 102 BGN for a new EV. A fee for used EVs was introduced for the first time, with about 180 BGN reported for EVs up to 5 years old; Capital says EV fees went up by up to 188 BGN. The most common imports over 10 years old went to 385 BGN. Individuals pay it at KAT registration, to PUDOOS or a recovery organisation. A bill to abolish the product fee at KAT registration, arguing that it was already paid in another member state for used EU cars, passed first reading in Sep 2026. Per Capital (30 Sep 2026) it still awaited second reading in the environment committee, and no final adoption was found.

- **This car:** This roughly 4-year-old BEV falls in the 'used EV up to 5 years' band. Expect about 180–188 BGN (about €92–96) unless the repeal is in force on your registration date. Check on the day.
- **Sources:** [capital.bg](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2025/05/30/4788888_ekotaksite_za_stari_koli_se_vdigat_sus_75_lv_za/), [banker.bg](https://banker.bg/2026/09/02/chast-chetene-deputatite-produktovata-taksa/), [banker.bg](https://banker.bg/2026/09/09/ekotaksite-za-avtomobilite-sporni-na-izhoda/), [capital.bg](https://www.capital.bg/politika_i_ikonomika/bulgaria/2026/09/30/4960892_upravliavashtite_s_novi_popravki_v_zakona_za/), [actualno.com](https://www.actualno.com/politics/da-otpadnat-ekotaksite-za-prevoznite-sredstva-db-vnese-zakonoproekt-news_2476107.html), [feabg.com](https://feabg.com/eko-taksa-za-avtomobili-2026-aktualni-tseni-i-promeni-v-naredbata/), [mvr.bg](https://mvr.bg/upload/21666/62a323c3-dokumenti_za_reg_mps2pdf.pdf)

### 11. Annual vehicle tax for an EV (medium confidence, sourced in this pass)

Several 2026 Bulgarian sources say fully electric cars pay €0 annual vehicle tax (данък МПС). One cites ЗМДТ art. 58 as a statutory exemption; others say it depends on the municipal ordinance and that all large municipalities (Sofia, Varna, Plovdiv, Burgas, Stara Zagora) grant it. The tax is paid to the municipality of the owner's permanent address. No legal text was fetched.

- **This car:** Expect €0 per year as a BEV. Confirm with the municipality of the buyer's permanent address.
- **Sources:** [danachenkalkulator.com](https://danachenkalkulator.com/blog-elektricheski-avtomobili-danaci), [goelectric.bg](https://www.goelectric.bg/blog/danachni-oblekchenia-elektromobili), [ekolata.com](https://ekolata.com/danak-za-ekologichen-avtomobil/), [karai.bg](https://karai.bg/instrumenti/danak-mps)

### 12. Municipal acquisition tax (данък при придобиване) – missing from round 1 (low confidence, sourced in this pass)

Bulgarian municipalities levy a tax on vehicles acquired for payment: 0.1–3% of the insurance value (one source says the higher of price and insurance value), with the rate set by each municipal council's ordinance. The vehicle must be declared to the municipality of the permanent address within 2 months. For a vehicle not previously registered in Bulgaria, that 2-month period runs from the date of registration. Proof that the acquisition tax was paid is part of the declaration file. No source confirmed how it applies to a car bought in another EU state, and none showed an EV exemption.

- **This car:** Possibly material: at 2–3% of a roughly €25–30k insurance value it would be several hundred euros. Ask the buyer's municipality before buying whether it applies to a used car imported from Romania and what the rate is.
- **Sources:** [minfin.bg](https://www.minfin.bg/bg/784), [egov.bg](https://egov.bg/wps/portal/egov/dostavchitsi+na+uslugi/obshtinski+administratsii/unificirani+uslugi/9402), [pravko.bg](https://www.pravko.bg/blog/kalkulator-notarialni-taksi-mps), [sofia.obshtini.bg](https://sofia.obshtini.bg/doc/385434), [anagami.bg](https://www.anagami.bg/blog/danak-lek-avtomobil-fizichesko-lice/)

### 13. Re-registration in the country of residence (EU) (high confidence, sourced in this pass)

Your Europe: a car is registered in the country where you live. Registration certificates must be recognised across the EU, and for re-registration the original certificate (both parts if it has two) is presented. The Council reached a general approach in Nov 2025 on a new vehicle-registration-documents directive that would repeal Directive 1999/37/EC; it is not yet in force.

- **This car:** A buyer resident in Bulgaria registers in Bulgaria. Hand KAT the original Romanian registration certificate together with the CIV, the document that plays the Part II role.
- **Sources:** [europa.eu](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/vehicles/registration/formalities/indexamp_bg.htm), [europa.eu](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/vehicles/registration/formalities/faq/indexamp_bg.htm), [data.consilium.europa.eu](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-15615-2025-ADD-2/bg/pdf)

## Corrections to the first pass

- Round 1 said EVs 'reportedly' pay no eco/product fee. That is wrong for 2026: since 1 Jul 2025 Bulgaria charges EVs a product fee (102 BGN new; about 180–188 BGN, roughly €92–96, reported for a used EV up to 5 years old). A repeal bill passed only first reading in Sep 2026 and was awaiting second reading on 30 Sep 2026 (capital.bg, banker.bg).
- Round 1 said a ГТП inspection is required before first registration of a used import. MVR's official first-registration page says something different. A valid roadworthiness document from another EU/EEA/Swiss state is presented, and if there is none or it has expired, the technical check is done at the 'Пътна полиция' registration point. A separate ГТП is therefore not clearly mandatory, although one secondary site claims it is for cars over 3 years old. Ask the local KAT.
- Round 1 framed Romanian provisional plates as limited to residents. The bigger problem is that the Romanian Border Police state red-plate authorisations are valid only on Romanian territory, so they cannot be used to drive the car into Bulgaria by anyone.
- Round 1 had no deadline for Bulgarian registration. ZDvP art. 140 (cited in the MVR service document) requires presenting the vehicle for registration within 1 month of acquisition.
- Round 1 left out the municipal acquisition tax (данък при придобиване, 0.1–3% of insurance value, set by each municipality; the 2-month declaration period runs from Bulgarian registration for a car not previously registered in Bulgaria). Whether it applies to an EU import is unconfirmed, but it could cost several hundred euros.
- Round 1 said a 'certified translation (likely)' was needed. The MVR document list uses the term 'legalized translation' (легализиран превод) for foreign documents and for the CoC, which is stricter than a plain certified translation.
- On Romanian deregistration: guides confirm the fiscal certificate comes from the local tax office (with taxes and fines paid). Proof of export can be the foreign sale contract (with a certified translation) or proof of registration abroad. Deregistration can proceed without the CIV and plates if they were kept by a foreign authority, which supports round 1's sequence B.
- One Romanian guide (carmatik.ro) claims export customs formalities at a Romanian customs office are obligatory. That is inconsistent with the EU customs union, which has no customs formalities for intra-EU movement of an EU-registered car; this is background knowledge, not verified this run. Do not plan for a customs export declaration for Romania to Bulgaria.
- Romania's 21% VAT is now verified, from Law 141/2025 effective 1 Aug 2025 and still 21% per 2026 guides. Round 1's €31.42 Bulgarian plate/certificate fee could NOT be re-verified this run.
