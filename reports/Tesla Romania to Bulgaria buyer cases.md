# One blog post maps the Bucharest-to-Bulgaria Tesla route

The evidence is thin. In this research I found only **one identifiable first-hand account** of a Bulgarian private buyer taking a new Tesla from Tesla Romania: a personal blog post on Kirichkov.com. Its date could not be verified and is probably 2024 or earlier. I found **no dated 2025–2026 account** that names a buyer, a month, a model, a VAT outcome and a transport method, for Romania or for any other EU Tesla market. Every source was read through search-engine snippets only, because WebFetch was blocked on tesla.com, teslamotorsclub.com, kirichkov.com, goelectric.bg, teslaowners.bg and the Bulgarian legal and government sites. What the evidence does show is consistent. Tesla Romania will sell to a Bulgarian. Its front-line practice has been to **charge Romanian VAT and refund it only after you prove you paid Bulgarian VAT**, and the refund needed "more pressure". You arrange transport yourself. You then declare the car at NAP, pay **20% Bulgarian VAT**, and register it at KAT using the invoice and COC. On a car listed at **€53,990 including 21% RO VAT**, the net price is about **€44,620**. Romanian VAT is about **€9,370** and Bulgarian VAT about **€8,924**. If Tesla RO charges VAT and refunds it later, you will have about **€62,900 committed for weeks or months**, and the €9,370 refund depends entirely on Tesla's internal VAT team. That cash-flow and counterparty risk is the main thing to plan around. A second factor applies for a Q1 2027 delivery: Tesla registered a Bulgarian subsidiary, Tesla BGR EOOD, in February 2026. That entity could change the route before your car arrives.

## The evidence base: one blog, two guides, old forum threads

Most of what circulates in Bulgarian about this route comes from the Kirichkov.com post "Можем ли да си купим нова Tesla от Букурещ, Румъния?" ("Can we buy a new Tesla from Bucharest, Romania?"). It says it is "entirely possible to buy a new, available Tesla from the showroom in Bucharest". It says the process is not as simple as buying from a local dealer, but not fundamentally different from buying in Austria or Germany. It also says it applies **only to a private individual without VAT registration buying for personal use** ([Kirichkov.com](https://kirichkov.com/959/%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BC-%D0%BB%D0%B8-%D0%B4%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%B8-%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BF%D0%B8%D0%BC-%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0-tesla-%D0%BE%D1%82-%D0%B1%D1%83%D0%BA%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%89-%D1%80/)). The site has a May 2024 archive and a "Romania" tag ([Kirichkov.com May 2024](https://kirichkov.com/date/2024/05/)), but I could not confirm the post's own date or read its comments, where later reader experiences may be.

The 2026 Bulgarian material consists of guides, not personal accounts. Goelectric.bg says "dozens of Bulgarians buy [Model Y] every month" directly from Tesla in Austria, Germany, Romania and Hungary. It says the easiest route is to visit the Bucharest showroom, where staff help you order and pay the deposit ([goelectric.bg](https://www.goelectric.bg/blog/tesla-model-y-bulgaria-2026)). The "dozens" figure is an unsourced estimate. Teslaowners.bg describes the same flow: configure the car, pick a delivery centre in Vienna, Bucharest, Budapest or Germany, pay the deposit, then **organise transport to Bulgaria yourself** ([teslaowners.bg Model Y guide](https://teslaowners.bg/tesla-model-y-%D0%B1%D1%8A%D0%BB%D0%B3%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%8F-%D0%BF%D1%8A%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BD-%D0%B3%D0%B8%D0%B4/)). Both guides repeat Kirichkov's deposit and VAT points almost word for word. That suggests they recycle one account rather than confirm it independently.

The rest is older evidence from Teslamotorsclub forum threads about Tesla Germany, Austria and the Netherlands, mostly from around 2019–2023. I found no Reddit, Facebook, YouTube or bg-mamma accounts from Bulgarians in 2025–2026. That may only mean those sources were not indexed or not reachable, so it does not prove no such accounts exist.

## Tesla Romania has billed VAT first and refunded it later

Romanian law allows a VAT-free invoice. Kirichkov's experience suggests Tesla's sales staff did not issue one. Under Romanian rules, a new means of transport sent to another member state is an exempt intra-community supply **even when the buyer has no VAT number**. The invoice is marked "scutit cu drept de deducere" (exempt with right of deduction) ([infotva.manager.ro](https://infotva.manager.ro/articole/infotva/tva-livrare-intracomunitara-mijloc-de-transport-4019.html); [ANAF Brașov leaflet](https://static.anaf.ro/static/10/Brasov/Brasov/AIC_mij_transport.pdf)). "New" means no more than 6 months since first entry into service or no more than 6,000 km, and the seller must document the transport, for example with freight papers showing the VIN ([BDO Latvia](https://www.bdo.lv/en-gb/insights/featured-insights/eu-delivery-of-new-vehicles-and-applicable-vat)). A general Romanian accounting blog says the opposite: that private EU buyers pay Romanian VAT. That is the general rule for distance sales, not the special rule for new vehicles ([SmartBill](https://blog.smartbill.ro/regim-tva-intracomunitar/)). Front-line staff may well rely on that general rule.

Kirichkov's advice, in snippet paraphrase, is to ask at order time for an invoice without VAT and to sign a declaration that the car will leave Romania immediately for registration in Bulgaria. If VAT is charged anyway, he says "they do refund it, it just takes a bit more pressure". He also reports that staff first claimed the buyer had to recover the money from the Romanian state, and that he used ANAF's electronic inquiry form to push the matter. The document he says Tesla needed for the refund was the **NAP certificate proving Bulgarian VAT was paid** ([Kirichkov.com](https://kirichkov.com/959/%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BC-%D0%BB%D0%B8-%D0%B4%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%B8-%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BF%D0%B8%D0%BC-%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0-tesla-%D0%BE%D1%82-%D0%B1%D1%83%D0%BA%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%89-%D1%80/)). An undated Tesla Club Bulgaria FAQ describes the same "pay twice, then claim back" pattern for private buyers in general ([teslaclub.bg FAQ](https://teslaclub.bg/category/faq/)).

Tesla's behaviour in other EU markets points the same way, and it shows a real downside. Tesla Netherlands once issued net invoices but took **a deposit equal to the VAT**. Tesla Germany "doesn't issue invoices without VAT". One buyer who submitted every document reported that "Tesla's VAT manager didn't authorize it", with no reason given ([TMC thread 161456](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/export-model-3-from-germany-and-vat-refund.161456/); [TMC thread 291529](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/buying-from-tesla-germany-from-outside-germany-i-e-export-to-another-eu-country.291529/)). A Tesla Graz employee reportedly said Tesla had **lost money when customers never returned export papers** ([TMC thread 174441](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/processing-vat-refund-from-tesla-germany.174441/)). That explains why Tesla now holds the money until it sees proof. No source covers how a Bulgarian VAT-registered company was treated. Under the normal rule, a company with a valid VIES number and proven RO→BG transport gets a 0% intra-community invoice ([fiscalitatea.ro](https://www.fiscalitatea.ro/vanzare-bunuri-in-ue-care-sunt-implicatiile-fiscale-18356/)).

The order contract adds a complication. Snippets of Tesla's European order agreement say the car should be **registered in the country shown in the agreement footer** at or before delivery. They also say Tesla may cancel orders it suspects are for resale, **including if the car is not registered within 7 days of delivery** ([Tesla Order Agreement, EU](https://www.tesla.com/en_EU/order/download-order-agreement?country=BG)). A Romanian-footer contract therefore needs the export arrangement agreed in writing. Older forum advice for Germany and Austria was to email Tesla and place the order explicitly "for export" ([TMC thread 291529](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/buying-from-tesla-germany-from-outside-germany-i-e-export-to-another-eu-country.291529/)).

### What the money looks like on your car

| Item | Amount (approx.) |
|---|---|
| List price incl. 21% RO VAT | €53,990 |
| Net price (÷1.21) | €44,620 |
| Romanian VAT, refundable if charged | €9,370 |
| Bulgarian VAT at 20% of net, plus any delivery charges in the taxable base | ≈€8,924+ |
| Final cost if the refund succeeds | ≈€53,544 + transport + fees |
| Peak cash committed under "pay RO VAT, then refund" | ≈€62,900 |

The refund is worth about €450 more than the Bulgarian VAT, because Romania's rate rose from 19% to **21% on 1 August 2025** ([TVR Info](https://tvrinfo.ro/romania-intra-in-august-cu-tva-de-21-si-accize-mai-mari-scumpiri-la-transport-comunicatii-si-alimente/)). Bulgarian VAT is charged on the full price including accessories and delivery charges ([Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/vehicles/cars/vat-buying-selling-cars/index_bg.htm)), so a transport fee that Tesla invoices increases the base. I found **no data on how long the refund takes**. EU business-refund timelines do not apply to a refund that a seller owes a private buyer ([Lovat](https://vatcompliance.co/blog/vat-refund-in-europe-2026-and-how-to-claim-vat-paid-in-another-eu-country)).

## Getting the car across the Danube is undocumented

No source describes a specific buyer's transport, its cost, or a problem at Giurgiu–Ruse. The constraints are known, though. Romanian sources say **Tesla does not transport cars**, and that a carrier collecting one must be a **Tesla-approved company** ([Softpedia forum](https://forum.softpedia.com/topic/1208944-cum-sa-comanzi-o-tesla-in-romania/); [mytes.ro](https://mytes.ro/ajutor/livrarea-comenzii/)). That line comes from a snippet and I could not verify it. Romanian provisional plates (numere provizorii: 13 RON authorisation plus 40 RON plates, up to 90 days, with RCA insurance) are a standard Romanian buyer tool. According to Bulgarian snippets, though, Romanian transit plates are **issued only to Romanian residents and companies and are not valid outside Romania** ([Kirichkov.com](https://kirichkov.com/959/%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%BC-%D0%BB%D0%B8-%D0%B4%D0%B0-%D1%81%D0%B8-%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BF%D0%B8%D0%BC-%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B0-tesla-%D0%BE%D1%82-%D0%B1%D1%83%D0%BA%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%89-%D1%80/); [goelectric.bg](https://www.goelectric.bg/blog/tesla-model-y-bulgaria-2026)).

That leaves two options. One is Bulgarian transit plates. Reportedly you need the ownership document (the Tesla invoice) and a document of origin. The "T" type, valid up to 30 days, cost **€40.90** in 2026 ([avtoekspert.com](https://avtoekspert.com/2026/01/05/cena-razhodi-registraciya-avtomobil/); [pravatami.bg](https://www.pravatami.bg/s/3968)). However, no source confirms that a private buyer can get them before the car is in Bulgaria. The other option is a car transporter. I found no published Bucharest–Sofia car-carrier price. The only figures were adjacent ones, such as **€280–385** for a Bucharest–Sofia passenger transfer ([daytrip.com](https://daytrip.com/landing/bucharest-to-sofia)), and those are not quotes. My inference is that a carrier with a CMR consignment note also produces the best transport evidence for Tesla's VAT team, which is a real advantage.

## The Bulgarian side is well defined in law but untested in personal accounts

At NAP, a private person who is not VAT-registered and acquires a new car from another EU state must declare it on **Appendix No. 19 to the ППЗДДС** at the NAP office for their permanent address and pay **20% VAT** ([kik-info.com](https://kik-info.com/stanovishta-na-nap/index.php?pageID=15); [Nula.bg](https://blog.nula.bg/oblagane-s-dds-pri-pokupka-na-avtomobil-kola/)). The sources disagree on the deadline. Some say 14 days from purchase ([LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B3%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B5-%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D1%84%D0%B8%D0%B7%D0%B8%D1%87%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BA%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B0-%D1%81-%D0%B4%D0%B4%D1%81-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8-%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BA%D1%83%D0%BF%D0%BA%D0%B0-%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B2-%D0%B0%D0%B2%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B8%D0%BB-%D0%BE%D1%82)). Another says 14 days after the end of the month in which VAT became chargeable ([ruskov-law.eu](https://ruskov-law.eu/sofia/article/danychno-tretirane-zakupuvane-avtomobil.html)). Plan for the stricter reading. Kirichkov notes that a certified copy of the filed declaration can take **up to 7 days** if requested electronically, and that copy is the document Tesla wants. I found no account of queues, extra attachments, or whether filing through the e-portal works for this form.

At KAT, registration guides list the following documents: the invoice (and contract, if there is one) in your name, the **COC**, MTPL insurance, ID, and the acquisition and eco-fee declarations. EVs are reported as exempt from eco-tax and annual vehicle tax ([bulcars.bg](https://www.bulcars.bg/%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B3%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%86%D0%B8%D1%8F-%D0%BD%D0%B0-%D0%B0%D0%B2%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%B1%D0%B8%D0%BB-%D0%B2-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D1%82/); [teslaowners.bg](https://teslaowners.bg/tesla-bgr-eood-balgariya-oficialno-predstavitelstvo/)). Registration costs about **€31.42** in 2026 ([avtoekspert.com](https://avtoekspert.com/2026/01/05/cena-razhodi-registraciya-avtomobil/)). Several points remain unresolved: whether KAT requires a certified translation of a Romanian or English invoice, how it checks NAP payment, whether a new car with a COC needs a technical inspection (the snippet saying so probably refers to used imports), and the rules for green EV plates.

The background could change before you take delivery. **Tesla BGR EOOD** was registered in Sofia on **18 February 2026**, owned by Tesla International B.V., and filed for sales and service activities on 28 April 2026 ([Drive Tesla Canada](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/tesla-bulgaria-expansion/); [digital.bg](https://digital.bg/tesla-naj-posle-vliza-v-balgariya-dokazatelstvoto-veche-e-fakt/)). As of August 2026 there was still no store or service centre in Bulgaria, and goelectric.bg forecast a Sofia location for late 2026 to mid-2027 ([goelectric.bg](https://www.goelectric.bg/blog/tesla-model-y-bulgaria-2026)). If a Bulgarian market setting opens before Q1 2027, the cross-border VAT problem could disappear.

## What to do based on these examples

1. **Before paying the deposit, get the VAT treatment in writing from Tesla RO.** Ask by email whether they will issue a VAT-exempt intra-community invoice for a new means of transport for a non-VAT-registered Bulgarian buyer (cite the "scutit cu drept de deducere" basis). If they will not, ask what refund conditions apply, which documents they need, and what the expected refund time is. Also ask them to confirm that the "register in footer country within 7 days" clause is waived for export.
2. **Offer to sign the export declaration up front**, as Kirichkov recommends: a statement that the car leaves Romania immediately for registration in Bulgaria.
3. **Budget for about €62,900 of cash committed** under the pay-and-refund scenario, and for €9,370 being tied up for an unknown period. Do not finance the Bulgarian VAT on the assumption that the refund will come back quickly.
4. **Prefer a Tesla-approved carrier with a CMR**, unless Tesla RO confirms in writing that it accepts Bulgarian transit plates plus a self-drive. Get the approved-carrier list and quotes early. Romanian provisional plates are not a realistic option for a Bulgarian resident.
5. **Prepare the NAP side before delivery.** Line up an accountant who has done an Appendix 19 filing before, check the current deadline wording, and plan to file and pay within days of delivery. Request the certified copy or certificate right away, since it is the key that unlocks the Tesla refund.
6. **Register at KAT promptly** with the invoice, COC, NAP proof, MTPL and ID. Call your local KAT office in advance about translation and technical inspection requirements.
7. **Send Tesla RO one complete refund package**: the CMR or transport proof, the NAP declaration and payment proof, and the Bulgarian registration certificate. Keep a written trail and escalate in writing. Use ANAF's inquiry form if staff say the refund is "the state's job".
8. **Re-check Tesla BGR's status before ordering and again before delivery.** If Tesla opens Bulgarian ordering, or lets Bulgaria-registered deliveries go through Bucharest under a Bulgarian contract, that removes most of the risk above.
9. **Keep the 6-month / 6,000 km "new vehicle" thresholds in mind.** They decide whether the intra-community treatment applies at all, so do not delay Bulgarian registration.
10. **Compare against a Bulgarian intermediary** such as newtesla.bg, which says it works with Tesla Germany and Austria ([newtesla.bg](https://newtesla.bg/en)). Its fee may be worth paying to avoid the VAT-refund exposure.

## Conclusion

The route works on paper and, by at least one account, in practice. The risk sits with Tesla's VAT handling, not with the Bulgarian state. NAP and KAT follow written rules that thousands of used-car importers use every year, while Tesla's refund decision is discretionary and opaque, and there is one documented refusal elsewhere in the EU. For a late-2026 order, the advice you can verify for yourself is not "copy what others did", because almost nobody has documented it. It is to turn Tesla RO's obligations into written commitments before money changes hands.

The timing also gives this buyer an option most earlier buyers did not have. A Q1 2027 delivery falls inside the window when Tesla BGR may start selling. A small deposit now, with the VAT terms confirmed in writing and the Bulgarian entity monitored until delivery, keeps the cheaper Romanian slot open without locking you into the refund gamble.
